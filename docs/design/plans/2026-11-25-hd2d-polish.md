# The Last Curtain: HD-2D polish (note 22) — plan

**Status:** proposed, for the user's review (2026-11-25). Nothing is built yet.

**Goal:** make the board and the battle view read like Octopath Traveler and Triangle Strategy: painted
figures in a lit miniature, shallow focus, warm lamplight against the dark, and a camera that tells
the fight. No new gameplay.

**What makes that look (references, not to be copied):**
- *Octopath Traveler (HD-2D):*
  - flat sprites standing in a 3D set;
  - tilt-shift depth of field, so the scene looks like a miniature;
  - strong bloom and point lights (lamps, fire);
  - god rays and dust in the air;
  - a vignette;
  - the camera close and low.
- *Triangle Strategy:*
  - the map as a diorama on a table, with height and edges;
  - the camera rotates in fixed steps;
  - skills cut in close, then return;
  - the order and the danger read at a glance.

**What Umbra already has:**
- post effects: bloom, vignette, tone and colour, CRT and VHS (`Post.md`);
- particles with pictures (dust, embers, ink);
- painted floors and rooms;
- lights (S6);
- sequences with camera cues (S55, S56);
- the 10-tile board view;
- the battle view with sides at 25% and 75%.

**What is missing:**
- depth of field;
- per-room lights that the figures catch;
- light shafts;
- the diorama edge;
- camera rotation;
- figure rim light and shadows that match the lamps.

## Decisions to make (please choose)

1. **How strong is the blur?** (a) Light: the top and bottom thirds go soft (recommended: it keeps
   the board readable). (b) Strong, as in Octopath: only the focused band is sharp.
2. **Can the camera rotate?** (a) No: keep one angle and make it beautiful (recommended for slice 1).
   (b) In four 90° steps, as in Triangle Strategy. That needs sprite backs or mirroring, and wall
   hiding.
3. **Is the board a diorama?** (a) Yes: the board stands on a dark plinth with a lit edge, and the
   void around it is black velvet (recommended). (b) No: rooms fade into fog.

## Tasks (in order; each one shippable alone)

1. **Depth of field (engine, `Post`).** A tilt-shift pass on the composite: blur by screen height
   away from a focus band. Settings: `dof`, `dofCentre`, `dofWidth`. The focus band follows the
   acting unit's screen height, so the sharp line is always where the action is. Only the 3D view
   is blurred; menus stay sharp. Keep `Post.hlsl` and the WGSL in step, and write the maths in
   `Post.md`.
2. **Lamps the figures catch.** Each room in `match-look.json` gets one warm point light (and the
   stage a cold one). Figures take a rim light from the nearest lamp, and soft round shadows under
   them fall away from it. Data only, plus a small change to the sprite surface.
3. **Light shafts and air.**
   - one god-ray quad per lit room (a soft additive cone, picture-based);
   - the existing room dust made denser near lamps;
   - all of it turned off by Hiệu ứng: Giảm.
4. **The diorama.**
   - a plinth under the board with a bevelled lit edge;
   - a black velvet ground;
   - the walls' tops shaded darker;
   - when the camera is close, the wall nearest it fades so figures never hide behind it.
5. **Camera direction (with the sequences).**
   - a small slow drift (breathing) while a unit thinks;
   - skill cut-ins: a sequence's `camera` cue may set `near`, `tilt` and `yaw` for that moment;
   - a 0.2 s ease-in on every move.
   - Data in `sequences.json`.
6. **Figure finish.** Each figure gets:
   - a 1–2 px dark outline (a shader pass);
   - a soft contact shadow;
   - a tiny bob while idle;
   - a lean on a hit.

   Dread keeps working on top.
7. **Battle view stage.**
   - the backdrop painting lit from its side;
   - floor reflections (a faint mirrored sprite at 15%);
   - an over-the-shoulder push on the attacker's first blow.
8. **Look presets and check.**
   - `match-look.json` gets `hd2d` values;
   - before-and-after captures (SwiftShader) for each step;
   - frame time measured on your PC before and after (the 60 FPS target).

**Out of scope:** new models, real-time shadows from every light, and camera rotation unless you
choose 2(b).

**Risks:**
- Blur makes small text and tile marks harder to read. Marks and labels are drawn after the blur.
- Every new pass costs GPU time on your laptop. Measure after tasks 1, 3 and 6.

**How it's verified:**
- captures in the cloud;
- your play check on Windows, with frame time and the Giảm setting.
